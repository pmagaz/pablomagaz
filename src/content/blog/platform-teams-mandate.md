Every company I have joined in the last decade had already tried to build a platform team. Most of them had the right engineers and the wrong agreement.

The pattern repeats: a group is carved out of product engineering, told to "reduce friction", and measured on nothing in particular. Six months later it is a ticket queue. The technology was never the constraint — the mandate was.

## A mandate is three sentences, not a strategy deck

A working mandate names the decisions the team owns, the decisions it does not, and the outcome it is accountable for. If you cannot write it in three sentences that a product director will repeat back to you unprompted, you do not have one yet.

> If the platform team can be overruled by any product manager with a deadline, it is not a platform team. It is a favour.

The uncomfortable part is that the mandate is not the platform team's to write. It belongs to the executive who owns delivery — usually the CTO. Delegating that authorship is the single most common failure I see, and the easiest to fix.

## What to do on Monday

Pick the three interfaces the platform will own outright for the next two quarters. Publish them. Then defend the first product team that is told no — publicly, and in the same week. Nothing communicates a mandate faster than a decision that holds.
