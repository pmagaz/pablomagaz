The org chart you sketch in month one is the product you ship in year two. Not because structure determines quality, but because structure determines which conversations happen without anyone scheduling them.

## Ten people is an architecture

Hire ten generalists and you get a system anyone can change and nobody owns. Hire ten specialists and you get clean boundaries and a queue at every one of them. Neither is wrong. Only one of them matches the bet you have actually made.

> Every reporting line is a guess about which decisions need to be cheap.

## The part that is hard to undo

Titles and levels set at ten people become the ceiling at fifty. So does the first senior hire who is tolerated rather than trusted. Compensation bands, review cadence and the definition of "senior" are all easier to set now than to correct later.

Write down the bet. Then hire the shape of the team that makes the bet cheap to test.
