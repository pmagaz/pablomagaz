Most build decisions are really scheduling decisions in disguise. The question is rarely "could we build this?" — it is "what else stops while we do?"

## The third option nobody writes down

Buy and build get compared on a slide. Wait almost never appears, even though it is frequently correct: the requirement is not stable yet, the vendor market is about to consolidate, or the team that would own it does not exist.

> If you cannot name what you are giving up to build it, you have not costed it.

## A defensible answer

Buy when the capability is undifferentiated and the integration is cheap. Build when it is the thing customers choose you for, and you can staff it permanently. Wait when the requirement is still moving.

Then write the reasoning down with a date on it. In eighteen months somebody will ask why, and the honest answer — "this was true then" — only works if it is on paper.
