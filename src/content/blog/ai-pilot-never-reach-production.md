A demo that impresses the board is not evidence of anything yet. It is evidence that the happy path works on curated inputs, which is the least interesting thing you can learn about a system.

## The gap the demo hides

Pilots are optimised for persuasion. Production is optimised for the tenth percentile day: the malformed input, the timeout, the customer who phrases the question in a way nobody anticipated. Between those two sits most of the engineering.

> The question is not "did it work?" but "what did we learn about the cases where it will not?"

## What earns a promotion to production

Three things, in order. A measurable definition of a wrong answer. A path for a human to intervene that is faster than the model. And an owner who is accountable for the output, not for the launch.

If a pilot cannot produce those, killing it is not a failure. It is the pilot doing its job.
