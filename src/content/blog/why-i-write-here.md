Most of what I know about this job I learned by getting it wrong first. That is not humility — it is just how the work goes. The decisions that matter most rarely come with enough information, and they almost never come with a second attempt.

## The Gap I Keep Running Into

There is a lot written about how to build software and very little about the position you are in when you have to decide *whether* to build it. The engineering blogs are excellent on architecture. The management books are fine on people. Almost nothing covers the middle, which is where a CTO actually spends the day: the moment when the technology decision and the business decision turn out to be the same decision.

That gap is where I keep finding myself, and where the conversations I have with other technical leaders keep landing.

## What I'll Be Writing About

Roughly four things, and probably in this proportion:

- **Leadership** — mandates, org design, and the decisions that are yours to make even when it would be more comfortable to delegate them.
- **Technology** — platform strategy, buy-versus-build, and the architecture calls that are really scheduling calls in disguise.
- **AI** — what survives contact with production, and what should have been killed at the pilot stage.
- **Mentoring** — the questions first-time CTOs ask me, and the answers I have changed my mind about.

Occasionally something more personal, filed under **Me**, when the lesson only makes sense with the context around it.

## How I'll Write It

Short, specific and with the uncomfortable part left in. I would rather publish one honest paragraph about a decision that went badly than a thousand words of framework.

I am also not going to pretend these are settled conclusions. Some of what I write here I will disagree with in two years. That is fine — I would rather leave a record with a date on it than nothing at all.

## Where This Goes

If something here is useful, argue with me about it. The best version of any of these ideas has always come out of a conversation with someone who had seen the same problem from a different chair.
